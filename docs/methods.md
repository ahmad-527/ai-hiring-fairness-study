# Methods

## Design and recruitment

The coursework used an online, within-person vignette survey. Participants read two hypothetical rejection scenarios, one attributed to a member of a recruitment team and one to an automated AI system. Presentation order was counterbalanced. Each scenario referred to a different company; ratings concern those scenarios rather than real employers.

Recruitment used convenience sampling with eligibility screening: adults who had applied for a job or internship in the previous 12 months and had fewer than five years of full-time professional experience. The report describes voluntary participation and informed consent. It does not claim institutional ethics approval.

## Sample flow

| Stage | Respondents |
| --- | ---: |
| Exported response records | 65 |
| Consented | 63 |
| Completed eligibility screening | 58 |
| Eligible | 41 |
| Completed all outcome items | 22 |
| Passed attention check: primary analysis | 19 |

The primary sample comprised 10 human-first and 9 AI-first participants. There are two scenario ratings per person; the inferential sample size is **19**, not the number of item responses.

## Measures and scoring

Procedural fairness used the seven-item procedural justice subscale from Colquitt (2001). Employer attractiveness used the five-item Company Attractiveness subscale from Highhouse, Lievens and Sinar (2003). Scenario stems were adapted in the coursework survey. Both used 1–5 responses; the second attractiveness item was reverse-scored once as `6 - response`.

Scores were unweighted item means for each outcome and condition. Primary analysis required all 24 outcome responses and a correct attention-check response. Differences were defined as human minus AI. In the primary sample, Cronbach's alpha ranged from 0.71 to 0.88 across the four scale/condition combinations. These estimates concern this small sample and do not establish that adapted wording has been independently validated.

## Analysis and sensitivity

The main comparisons used two-sided paired t-tests, 95% confidence intervals for mean paired differences and Cohen's dz. Holm adjustment covered the two primary outcomes. The report also includes Wilcoxon checks, presentation-order comparisons and sensitivity analyses.

The published aggregate CSV contains the primary sample, all eligible completers including attention-check failures (N = 22), and the subset of the primary sample passing both reviewer-identification checks (N = 12). The latter is a post-scenario subset and should not replace the primary analysis. Sensitivity-sample p-values in the CSV are unadjusted; Holm-adjusted p-values are provided only for the two primary outcomes.

The report's additional exploratory exclusion of possible repeated screener attempts (N = 17) was not rechecked for this portfolio and is not included in the CSV. Its figures and full literature review have not been independently replicated here.

## Data availability

Raw survey exports contain platform-generated technical metadata, including IP addresses, approximate coordinates and response identifiers. They remain private. This repository distributes only outcome-level aggregate statistics and sample counts. The report's original end-to-end analysis script was not located among the available project files; a separate private aggregate check was created for the portfolio. No full public reproduction from respondent-level data is provided.
